\begin{theorem}[Arcsine central limit theorem for a Bernoulli mean]
Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ be a probability space, let $0<\theta<1$, and
let $(X_i)_{i\in\mathbb N}$ be measurable, mutually independent
$\{0,1\}$-valued random variables with Bernoulli distribution of success
probability $\theta$. Let $Z:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ be measurable and have
the standard normal distribution. For $r\in\mathbb N$, define
\[
 \overline X_r(\omega)=\frac1{r+1}\sum_{i=0}^r X_i(\omega),
\]
where a successful Boolean outcome is identified with $1$ and a failure
with $0$. Then
\[
 2\sqrt{r+1}\left(
 \arcsin\sqrt{\overline X_r}-\arcsin\sqrt\theta
 \right)
 \xrightarrow{\mathcal D} Z
 \quad\text{under }P\text{ as }r\to\infty.
\]
\end{theorem}
