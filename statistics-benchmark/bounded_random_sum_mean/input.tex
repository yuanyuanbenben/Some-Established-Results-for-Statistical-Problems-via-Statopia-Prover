\begin{theorem}[Mean of a bounded random sum]
Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ be a probability space and let $m\geq1$. Let
$N:\Omega\to\mathbb N$ be measurable and satisfy $N(\omega)\leq m$ for
every $\omega$. Let $X_0,\ldots,X_{m-1}:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ be measurable
and integrable, and suppose that $\mathbb E_P[X_i]=\mu$ for every $i$.
Assume that $N$ is independent of the random vector
$(X_0,\ldots,X_{m-1})$. Define
\[
 S(\omega)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}
 \begin{cases}
 X_i(\omega),&i+1\leq N(\omega),\\
 0,&i+1>N(\omega),
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 N_{\mathbb R}(\omega)=N(\omega)\ \text{viewed as a real number}.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathbb E_P[S]=\mu\,\mathbb E_P[N_{\mathbb R}].
\]
\end{theorem}
