\begin{theorem}[Eventual recovery of a unique Fr\'echet mean on a finite space]
Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ be a probability space.  Let
$(\mathcal X,d)$ be a nonempty finite metric space equipped with a linear
order and with a measurable structure in which every singleton is
measurable.  Let $X_i:\Omega\to\mathcal X$, $i\ge0$, be measurable,
jointly independent random variables, each having the same distribution as
$X_0$.  Let $\mu\in\mathcal X$ satisfy, for every $z\ne\mu$,
\[
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}P\{X_0=x\}\,d(\mu,x)^2
 <
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}P\{X_0=x\}\,d(z,x)^2,
\]
where each probability is interpreted as its real value.  For each
$n\ge0$, let $\widehat\mu_n:\Omega\to\mathcal X$ be measurable.  Assume
that, for every $n\ge1$, every $\omega$, and every $z\in\mathcal X$,
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d(\widehat\mu_n(\omega),X_i(\omega))^2
 \le
 \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d(z,X_i(\omega))^2,
\]
and assume the smallest-minimizer rule: whenever the two displayed sums are
equal, $\widehat\mu_n(\omega)\le z$.  Then
\[
 P\left\{\omega:\text{there is an }N\ge1\text{ such that }
   \widehat\mu_n(\omega)=\mu\text{ for every }n\ge N\right\}=1.
\]
\end{theorem}
